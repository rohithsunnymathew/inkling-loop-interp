For a weeknight curry, whole spices such as cumin seeds, cardamom pods and cinnamon bark are toasted in hot oil until fragrant, then onion, ginger and garlic are fried until golden. Ground turmeric, coriander and chilli powder are added with a splash of water to stop them scorching. Chicken thighs are browned in the spice base, then simmered with tomatoes and coconut milk until tender. A final handful of fresh coriander and a spoon of yoghurt balance the heat.
Desserts reward precision. For a sponge cake, butter and sugar are creamed until pale and fluffy, eggs are beaten in one at a time, and sifted flour is folded in gently to keep the air in the batter. The tins are greased and lined, the oven is preheated, and the door stays closed for the first twenty minutes so the cake does not collapse. A skewer inserted into the centre should come out clean, and the cakes cool on a wire rack before being filled with whipped cream and strawberries.
Good stock is the foundation of many soups. Roasted chicken bones, onion, carrot, celery, peppercorns and parsley stalks are covered with cold water and brought slowly to a bare simmer, never a hard boil, which would make the stock cloudy. Skimming the foam from the surface during the first hour keeps it clear. After several hours the stock is strained, chilled, and the fat that sets on top is lifted off before the stock is used for risotto, gravy or a simple noodle soup.
Seasoning should happen throughout cooking rather than only at the end. Tasting as you go lets you adjust salt, acidity, sweetness and heat, and a dish that tastes flat often needs acid such as vinegar or citrus rather than more salt. Fresh herbs with tender leaves, like basil and dill, are added at the end, while woody herbs such as rosemary and thyme can cook for longer. Resting meat after roasting lets the juices redistribute so that it stays moist when sliced.
Breakfast can be just as careful. Eggs scrambled slowly over low heat with butter stay creamy, while high heat makes them rubbery. Pancake batter should rest so the flour hydrates, and lumps are fine because overmixing toughens the pancakes. A hot, lightly oiled pan gives an even golden colour, and the pancakes are flipped once bubbles form on the surface and the edges look set, then served with maple syrup, berries and a little honey.
